# Industry and audience playbook

**A nudge, not a rule.** Each block is a starting point for a first conversation and a first direction: what this audience usually needs to see, what every competitor already does, and where there is room to stand out. The brief, the brand and the client's own material always win. If a client wants to break their sector's conventions on purpose, that is often the most interesting brief of the year.

Default craft and motion levels are suggestions for `PROJECT.md`; the kickoff intake in `tahi-core:tahi-project` sets the real ones. Motion levels are defined in `tahi-design:site-animator`.

Each block: **Expect** (what the audience needs to find), **Clichés** (what everyone else does), **Room** (where to be distinctive), **Levels** (default craft / motion), **Direction** (loose type, colour, imagery).

### SaaS and B2B tech
- **Expect:** what it does in one line, the real product, pricing or a clear route to it, integrations, security and compliance (SOC 2, ISO 27001, data residency), customer proof by role and company size, docs and changelog.
- **Clichés:** purple gradients, centred hero with two buttons, fake dashboard, three icon cards, logo strip, "all-in-one platform", bento grids.
- **Room:** show the actual product doing one real job; a live or interactive demo; specific numbers from named customers; a point of view on the problem; a voice with personality.
- **Levels:** craft 2 (3 for launches) / motion 2.
- **Direction:** a confident sans or grotesk with a distinctive display cut; brand colour plus strong neutrals; real UI crops, product-in-context photography, diagrams that explain.

### Professional services (legal, accounting, consulting)
- **Expect:** areas of practice, who the people are (named, photographed, with credentials), how to engage and what it costs or how fees work, location and contact, insights that prove expertise, regulatory details where required.
- **Clichés:** gavels, scales, handshakes, city skylines, navy and gold, "trusted advisors", stock boardrooms, partner headshots against grey.
- **Room:** real people photographed well in their real space; plain-English explanations of hard problems; fee transparency; specific outcomes; a warmer palette than the sector norm.
- **Levels:** craft 2 / motion 1.
- **Direction:** a serif or humanist sans with authority, generous reading type; restrained palette with one considered accent; editorial portrait photography.

### Finance and fintech
- **Expect:** what the product is and who regulates it, fees and rates up front, security, how to get help, disclosures and licences (in NZ, FSP registration and the FMA where relevant), calculators that work.
- **Clichés:** blue everything, glowing cards, people smiling at phones, coins and upward arrows, "take control of your finances".
- **Room:** honest numbers presented beautifully; a working calculator as the hero; clarity as the brand; a colour that is not blue.
- **Levels:** craft 2 / motion 1 (fintech consumer launches 2).
- **Direction:** clear sans with tabular figures; calm palette with high contrast; product UI and real customers rather than stock.

### Health and medical
- **Expect:** services, practitioners and their qualifications, how to book, cost and funding (in NZ, ACC, Te Whatu Ora funding, insurance), location, accessibility of the clinic, urgent-care guidance, privacy.
- **Clichés:** stethoscopes, teal and white, smiling models in scrubs, abstract cross shapes, hands holding hearts.
- **Room:** real practitioners and real rooms; calm, plain language; booking that takes two clicks; warmth without being saccharine.
- **Levels:** craft 2 / motion 1. Risk is often 3 if bookings store health information.
- **Direction:** highly readable humanist sans, large body text; soft but high-contrast palette; real environment photography.

### Wellness, beauty and fitness
- **Expect:** treatments or classes with prices and durations, timetable, booking, practitioners, before and after (where ethical and allowed), location and parking, gift vouchers.
- **Clichés:** sage green and beige, stones and orchids, serif-italic headlines, influencer stock, "your journey to wellness".
- **Room:** the actual space, texture and light; a timetable that is a pleasure to use; a distinct personality (clinical, playful, raw, luxurious) rather than generic calm.
- **Levels:** craft 2 to 3 / motion 2.
- **Direction:** expressive display type with a quiet text face; palette from the space's materials; close, tactile photography.

### Hospitality, food and venues
- **Expect:** menu with prices (as HTML, not a PDF), opening hours, location and map, booking, functions and private dining, dietary information, photos of the food and room.
- **Clichés:** full-screen video of pouring wine, chalkboard fonts, menus as PDFs, "a culinary journey".
- **Room:** the menu as the hero; hours and booking in the first screen; the street, the building and the people; seasonal changes that keep the site alive.
- **Levels:** craft 2 to 3 / motion 2.
- **Direction:** type that matches the room's signage and menus; colours from the interior and the plate; generous, honest food photography.

### Tourism and accommodation
- **Expect:** where it is and how to get there, rooms or experiences with real photos, rates and availability, what is included, booking engine, cancellation policy, reviews from real platforms, seasonality.
- **Clichés:** drone shot of the landscape with a centred script logo, "escape the everyday", stock couples in robes.
- **Room:** the specific place: its weather, its walks, its distances ("eight minutes to the lake"); honest room photography; a booking flow that does not hand off to a broken widget.
- **Levels:** craft 2 to 3 / motion 2.
- **Direction:** display type with a sense of place; palette from the landscape; large, place-specific photography and maps.

### Real estate and property
- **Expect:** listings with search and filters, price or price guidance, floor plans, location, agent contact, open homes, appraisal request, market insight.
- **Clichés:** blue and white corporate, aerial city shots, "your dream home awaits", agent headshots with arms crossed.
- **Room:** neighbourhood stories; floor plans and data shown well; development sites with real construction progress; an agent's local knowledge as content.
- **Levels:** craft 2 (3 for premium developments) / motion 1 to 2.
- **Direction:** clean architectural sans; restrained neutrals with one accent; professional property photography, plans and maps.

### Construction, trades and industrial
- **Expect:** services, service area, licences and accreditations (in NZ, LBP, Master Builders, Site Safe), real projects with photos, health and safety record, how to get a quote, phone number on every page.
- **Clichés:** hard hats, hi-vis stock, orange and black, blueprints, "quality you can trust".
- **Room:** real jobs, real crews, before-and-after; project detail that shows competence; an honest, direct voice; a quote form that asks the right questions.
- **Levels:** craft 1 to 2 / motion 1.
- **Direction:** sturdy sans or condensed type; brand colours from the fleet and uniforms; documentary project photography.

### Architecture and interiors
- **Expect:** the work, large and well photographed; process; the people; awards and publications; how to start a project.
- **Clichés:** white minimal grid, tiny grey text, monospace captions, project titles in uppercase tracking, hairline rules everywhere.
- **Room:** let the work lead but give each project a story (brief, site, constraint, response); a navigation that feels like moving through a building; readable type.
- **Levels:** craft 3 / motion 2.
- **Direction:** refined sans or serif with real size contrast; palette from materials; full-bleed photography, drawings and plans.

### Education
- **Expect:** courses or programmes with entry requirements, fees and dates, how to enrol, outcomes, campus and student life, international student information (in NZ, Code of Practice and NZQA details where relevant), support services.
- **Clichés:** students laughing on lawns, graduation caps, "shape your future", stock lecture halls.
- **Room:** real student work and voices; course finders that actually help; honest outcomes; a confident institutional voice.
- **Levels:** craft 2 / motion 1 to 2 (2 for younger audiences).
- **Direction:** highly readable type with a distinctive display; institutional colours used with restraint; real campus and student photography.

### Non-profit and charity
- **Expect:** the cause in one line, what a donation does, impact with real numbers, how to donate, volunteer and get involved, financial transparency (in NZ, Charities Services registration), stories.
- **Clichés:** sad stock imagery, hands in a circle, "make a difference today", green and orange, donate button everywhere with no context.
- **Room:** specific stories with dignity; impact as clear data; a donation flow that says exactly where money goes; hope over guilt.
- **Levels:** craft 2 / motion 1.
- **Direction:** warm, readable type; brand colour plus calm neutrals; documentary photography with consent.

### Government and public sector
- **Expect:** tasks first (find, apply, pay, report), plain language, accessibility at WCAG 2.2 AA minimum, search, contact, te reo Māori where appropriate, standards compliance (in NZ, the NZ Government Web Standards; in AU, the Digital Service Standard).
- **Clichés:** generic stock, heavy banners, PDFs for everything, deep navigation trees.
- **Room:** being genuinely useful; clarity and speed are the design; a well-made content design system.
- **Levels:** craft 1 to 2 / motion 0 to 1.
- **Direction:** system or highly legible sans, large body; accessible palette; functional imagery only.

### Creative, agency and portfolio
- **Expect:** the work first, who you are and how you work, clients and outcomes, contact. A site that is itself evidence of craft.
- **Clichés:** giant cursor followers, "we craft digital experiences", overlong preloaders, monospace micro-labels, local time and coordinates in the header, case studies with no results.
- **Room:** a real idea from the studio's identity; case studies with the problem and the result; a site that is fast as well as rich.
- **Levels:** craft 3 / motion 3.
- **Direction:** anything, as long as it is decided; expressive type; the work as the imagery.

### Fashion, retail and DTC ecommerce
- **Expect:** product imagery that sells (multiple angles, on body, detail), price, sizing and fit, delivery and returns in plain terms, stock status, reviews, fast checkout, afterpay-style options where offered.
- **Clichés:** the same Shopify theme layout, centred sale banner, "elevate your style", full-screen hero carousel.
- **Room:** editorial storytelling around the product; a strong art direction carried into the product grid; fit tools; a brand voice on every page, not only the homepage.
- **Levels:** craft 3 / motion 2 to 3 (keep product and checkout pages at the calmer end).
- **Direction:** brand-led type; colour from the collection; campaign and product photography with one consistent treatment.

### Events and entertainment
- **Expect:** what, when and where in the first screen; line-up or programme; tickets and price; venue, access and transport; FAQs; sponsors.
- **Clichés:** neon gradients, countdown timers, glitch effects, "the event of the year".
- **Room:** the programme as the design; the event's own visual identity carried through; a ticket flow that is fast on a phone.
- **Levels:** craft 2 to 3 / motion 2 to 3.
- **Direction:** loud display type if the brand is loud; high-contrast palette; real past-event photography and artist imagery.

## Audience modifiers

These cut across industries. Apply them on top of the industry block.

- **Older audiences.** Body 18px or more, high contrast, clear labels on every control, no hidden navigation on desktop, motion 1 at most, phone number visible. Avoid icon-only buttons.
- **Younger audiences.** Higher tolerance for motion and bold type (motion 2 to 3), mobile first in every decision, social proof from peers, short copy, fast pages on mobile data.
- **Technical buyers.** Specifics over adjectives: specs, docs, API, architecture diagrams, pricing details, security pages. Respect their time; no gated content for basics.
- **Enterprise procurement.** Security and compliance pages, case studies by company size and sector, accessibility statement, downloadable one-pagers, clear contact for sales. Calm motion (1 to 2).
- **Local and regional NZ and AU audiences.** Local addresses and phone numbers, NZ or AU spelling throughout, local imagery and place names, te reo Māori used correctly and with care where the client is comfortable, prices in local currency with GST stated, local proof (clients they will recognise). Do not use Americanised stock.
- **Accessibility-sensitive audiences** (disability services, health, ageing, government). WCAG 2.2 AA as a floor, often AAA contrast for body text; motion 0 to 1; no autoplay; plain-language summaries; tested with a screen reader and keyboard; an on-site motion toggle.
